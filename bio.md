Session 01 — Father’s Garage

#1960s #Minnesota #Family #Garage #Childhood #WorkEthic

— My father fixed engines almost every evening after work.

— The garage was always cold in winter and heavy with heat in summer.

— Everything had its place. Every tool, every bolt.

— If something moved, he noticed immediately.

— Not angry. Just aware.

— He didn’t talk much while working.

— But sometimes he’d say one sentence that stayed with you for years.

— “Listen to the machine. It tells you first.”

— I still hear that when I work.

⸻

Session 02 — Mother Helen

#1960s #Mother #Home #Family #DailyLife

— My mother made everything feel stable.

— She worked mornings at a diner.

— Knew half the town by their coffee order.

— Sundays were always pancakes and old records.

— Sinatra, mostly.

— She worried, but quietly.

— Never made it heavier for anyone else.

— That’s something I only understood later.

⸻

Session 03 — Sam

#1970s #Brother #Youth #RoadTrips #Music

— My brother Sam was the opposite of me.

— Loud, fast, always moving.

— He could fix a guitar and break a car in the same day.

— He didn’t plan much.

— Things just happened around him.

— We once drove through three states with almost no money.

— Ate gas station food for two days.

— He thought it was normal.

— Maybe it was.

⸻

Session 04 — Lake Minnesota

#1960s #Fishing #Grandfather #Nature #Silence

— My grandfather taught me fishing early.

— He didn’t like talking on the water.

— Said it changed the rhythm.

— Early mornings were the best.

— Mist over the lake, almost no sound.

— Just boat engine and coffee in metal cups.

— He used to say: “You learn patience before you learn skill.”

Session 05 — First Workshop Work

#1970s #Work #Engineering #Shipyard #Seattle

— Seattle was the first place that felt like real industry to me.

— Everything was noise and movement.

— Metal, steam, electricity.

— Shipyards don’t have silence.

— But they have rhythm.

— You start recognizing problems by sound.

— Before you even see them.

— That’s when I realized I liked fixing things more than building them.

⸻

Session 06 — Linda

#1980s #Love #Marriage #Seattle #Family

— I met Linda in a grocery store.

— I dropped a jar of pickles.

— She laughed.

— That was it.

— She worked in a bookstore.

— Read constantly.

— Our apartment had books and tools mixed everywhere.

— She tried to bring plants into my life.

— I accidentally killed most of them.

— She said I respected machines more than living things.

— She wasn’t wrong.

⸻

Session 07 — Emily

#1980s #Daughter #Family #Fatherhood

— When Emily was born, everything shifted.

— I stopped thinking in terms of work first.

— She asked questions constantly.

— About everything.

— Boats, rain, sound, light.

— I liked explaining things to her.

— We built a birdhouse once.

— Took all weekend.

— She painted it bright blue.

— Completely ignored my measurements.

— I think it was better that way.

⸻

Session 08 — Alaska

#1980s #Alaska #Work #FishingBoats #Cold

— Alaska felt unreal the first time.

— Too big, too quiet.

— Wind hits differently there.

— Sharp, immediate.

— We worked on fishing vessels.

— Long shifts, cold hands, hot coffee.

— One coworker cooked chili so spicy nobody could finish it.

— He brought it every Friday anyway.

⸻

Session 09 — Family Cabin

#1990s #Family #Cabin #Nature #Tradition

— We used to go to the same cabin every summer.

— Same lake, same dock.

— Emily collected stones.

— Linda read outside.

— I fixed whatever broke first.

— Nights were quiet.


— Water, wind, distant music.

— I don’t think I ever fully learned how to just sit still.

⸻



Session 10 — Roads

#1990s #RoadTrips #America #Music #Memory

— Driving used to feel like freedom.

— Long roads, no phones.

— Just maps and instincts.

— Diners at night.

— Gas stations in the middle of nowhere.

— Music on the radio shaped the trip.

— Sometimes Linda slept while I drove.

— Those were good hours.

⸻

Session 11 — Work That Never Stopped

#2000s #Work #Tools #Community #Workshop

— People kept asking when I’d stop working.

— I never really started or stopped.

— I just fixed things.

— Lawn mowers, engines, boats.

— Neighbors brought things and sometimes pie.

— I liked that system.

— It made sense.

⸻

Session 12 — Today

#2020s #Aging #Family #Routine #Peace

— Mornings are quiet now.

— Coffee outside.

— Emily calls every week.

— She lives in Portland.

— Still asks technical questions.

— Linda says I answer too seriously.

— I think clarity matters.

— Even in conversation.

— Life feels like smaller routines now.

— But still connected.

— That’s enough.
